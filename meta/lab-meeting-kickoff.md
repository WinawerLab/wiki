# Lab meeting: launching the new wiki

A plan for one lab meeting (about 60 minutes). The goal is not to finish the wiki. It is for
everyone to make their first edit, own at least one page, and have a say in the pages about how
we work.

## Before the meeting (Jon)

- [ ] Make sure every current lab member is on the `winawerlab` team in the WinawerLab GitHub
      organization (the team has **Write** access to the `wiki` repository). Ask people without a
      GitHub account to make one before the meeting.
- [ ] Fill in the two "Jon to fill in" boxes: [Who does what](../docs/how-we-work/roles.md)
      and "How long things take" in [Authorship](../docs/how-we-work/authorship.md).
      Even rough answers help; they set the tone for the discussion.
- [ ] Ask everyone to bring a laptop.

## 1. Why a new wiki, and why it will be different (5 min)

- The old wiki had 81 pages. Most were out of date and nobody could tell which.
- This one starts small. Every page has an owner and a "last checked" date. Old pages show a
  yellow banner. A weekly check reminds owners.
- Show a page with a yellow banner (for example *Leaving the lab*) and the
  [Wiki health](https://winawerlab.github.io/wiki/about/health/) page.

## 2. Everyone's first edit (10 min)

Each person adds themselves to `people.yml`:
open https://github.com/WinawerLab/wiki/edit/main/people.yml, add an entry, and commit.
This proves everyone can edit, and it is what lets the wiki recognize page owners.

## 3. Clear the yellow banners (10 min)

Five pages came from the old wiki and are marked as out of date. Split them up. For each:
read it, fix what is wrong, set `reviewed:` to today, and put a new owner.

- Joining the lab (best checked by the newest lab member)
- Leaving the lab
- Human subjects and the IRB (each project lead adds their row to the protocol table)
- Places and contacts
- Where data and files live

## 4. How we work (20 min)

Go through the questions on the three draft pages together:
[Lab culture](../docs/how-we-work/culture.md), [Who does what](../docs/how-we-work/roles.md),
[Authorship](../docs/how-we-work/authorship.md). One person types answers into the page during
the discussion. Unresolved questions can stay on the page for next time.

These are the pages most likely to matter to people, so it is worth hearing everyone.

## 5. Triage the old wiki (10 min)

Open [the triage list](old-wiki-triage.md). 30 old pages need a decision (keep, link, or drop).
Work in pairs, one section each. A page is only kept if someone volunteers to own it.

## 6. Wrap up (5 min)

- Everyone owns at least one page.
- Pick a **wiki steward** for this semester: the person who glances at the weekly
  "Wiki freshness report" issue and nudges people. Rotate each semester.
- Put a 10-minute "wiki check" on the lab meeting agenda once a month.
