# Authorship and publishing

The first part of this page is a draft for the lab to discuss. The last part
([Required acknowledgments](#required-acknowledgments)) is checked and ready to use.

## Who is an author?

Many journals follow the four criteria of the
[ICMJE](https://www.icmje.org/recommendations/browse/roles-and-responsibilities/defining-the-role-of-authors-and-contributors.html)
(International Committee of Medical Journal Editors). An author should meet **all four**:

1. Made a substantial contribution to the idea or design of the work, **or** to collecting,
   analyzing, or interpreting the data.
2. Drafted the paper or revised it critically for important content.
3. Approved the final version.
4. Agreed to be accountable for the work.

People who helped but do not meet all four go in the acknowledgments.

Many journals also ask for a **contributor statement** using
[CRediT](https://credit.niso.org/), a list of 14 named roles (for example "Software",
"Formal analysis", "Writing – review & editing"). It is easier to keep track of roles as the
project goes than to reconstruct them at the end.

!!! question "Questions for the lab to answer"
    1. **When do we talk about authorship?** Suggestion: at the start of a project, and
       again whenever someone's role changes. Who writes it down, and where?
    2. **Author order.** How do we decide who is first author, and when are joint first
       authors appropriate? Where does Jon usually go?
    3. **People who leave.** If someone leaves before the paper is written, what happens to
       their project and their place on the paper?
    4. **Collaborators and core staff.** When do people outside the lab become authors?
    5. **Preprints.** Do we post a preprint before submitting? (Most of our recent papers did.)

## How long things take

Writing a paper takes longer than anyone expects. Being explicit about timing helps both
sides plan.

## Required acknowledgments

Some resources require a specific citation or sentence in every paper that uses them.
Add to this list when you learn of one.

### CMRR multiband fMRI sequences (CBI scanner)

Papers, abstracts and talks that use the CMRR multiband (simultaneous multi-slice) EPI
sequences must acknowledge that the software came from the University of Minnesota's CMRR,
and cite these three papers. See the
[CBI policies page](https://sites.google.com/nyu.edu/cbi-nyu/mri-lab/policies-procedures)
for the current wording.

- Moeller S, Yacoub E, Olman CA, Auerbach E, Strupp J, Harel N, Uğurbil K (2010).
  Multiband multislice GE-EPI at 7 tesla, with 16-fold acceleration using partial parallel
  imaging with application to high spatial and temporal whole-brain fMRI.
  *Magnetic Resonance in Medicine* 63(5):1144–1153.
- Feinberg DA, Moeller S, Smith SM, Auerbach E, Ramanna S, Glasser MF, Miller KL,
  Uğurbil K, Yacoub E (2010). Multiplexed echo planar imaging for sub-second whole brain
  fMRI and fast diffusion imaging. *PLoS ONE* 5(12):e15710.
- Xu J, Moeller S, Auerbach EJ, Strupp J, Smith SM, Feinberg DA, Yacoub E, Uğurbil K (2013).
  Evaluation of slice accelerations using multiband echo planar imaging at 3 T.
  *NeuroImage* 83:991–1001.

### NYU high performance computing (HPC)

If the work used the NYU HPC cluster, include this sentence:

> This work was supported in part through the NYU IT High Performance Computing resources,
> services, and staff expertise.
