# Human subjects and the IRB

Before you test any participant, three things must be true:

1. You have finished **human subjects training** (CITI).
2. You are listed as study personnel on the **IRB protocol** that covers your study.
3. You know which **consent forms** go with that protocol.

## 1. Training (CITI)

Every new lab member who will run participants completes the CITI course.
NYU's instructions are on the
[human subjects research page](https://www.nyu.edu/research/resources-and-support-offices/getting-started-withyourresearch/human-subjects-research.html).
Send Jon your completion certificate.

## 2. Getting added to a protocol

Jon adds you to the protocol by filing an amendment with the IRB. To do that, you must be
registered in NYU's **Cayuse** research system.

To check: in NYU Home, go to *Research → eResearch Toolkit → Cayuse Research Suite*.

- If you see a list of modules, you are registered. Tell Jon.
- If you get an error about access, request registration at
  [nyu.edu/osp/res/registration](https://www.nyu.edu/osp/res/registration/) and tick
  "Register for Cayuse". You will get an email when it is done. Then tell Jon.

Graduate students and postdocs should also get an **NIH eRA Commons ID**.

## The Declaration of Helsinki

Some journals (for example *Journal of Vision*, published by ARVO) ask whether a study follows
the [Declaration of Helsinki](https://www.wma.net/policies-post/wma-declaration-of-helsinki/).
NYU's IRB does not review studies against the Declaration. It applies the US "Common Rule",
which is similar but not identical. In 2021, NYU's human research compliance director advised
reading the Declaration yourself and deciding whether your study follows it. Billy Broderick
read it in September 2021 and judged that it applies to our studies without problems. It is
still worth checking for your own study.
